# 📈 What Makes a Top Performer?
# An HR Analytics Deep Dive into Employee Performance, Retention, and Burnout

[What Makes A Top Performer? An HR Data Deep Dive](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/madlene.oloo/viz/MindtheGapWorkboard/Dashboard3)

## 🌍 Overview

What separates a top performer from a low performer?

Most organizations measure employee performance, yet few truly understand what drives it. Performance reviews often identify who is succeeding and who is struggling, but they rarely explain why. As a result, companies risk losing talented employees, investing in ineffective interventions, and allowing preventable turnover to grow unnoticed.

This project explores employee performance using an interactive Tableau dashboard built from the Employee Performance and Productivity Dataset. Rather than simply reporting performance metrics, the dashboard investigates the structural factors that shape employee success—from training opportunities and job satisfaction to workload, promotion patterns, and compensation.

The goal is not just to identify high performers, but to understand what creates them—and what happens when organizations fail to invest in their people.

## 🚨 Why This Matters

Poor performance is often treated as an individual problem.

In reality, it is frequently an organizational one.

Employees who appear to be underperforming may be undertrained, overlooked, or working within departments that lack the resources needed for success. Likewise, some of the organization's highest performers may quietly burn out because their effort goes unrecognized.

Without understanding these patterns, organizations risk:

- Losing experienced employees through preventable attrition
- Investing training resources inefficiently
- Reducing productivity across key departments
- Increasing recruitment and onboarding costs
- Creating cultures where effort is not rewarded

This project uses data to uncover those hidden patterns before they become costly business problems.

## 🎯 Business Question

What separates a top performer from a low performer, and what does it cost an organization when that gap goes unaddressed?

## 🔍 Analytical Approach

The dashboard follows a narrative-driven analytical journey that examines employee performance from multiple perspectives.

Part I — The Performance Story

The first section explores organizational performance by asking:

- What is the overall health of the workforce?
- Which departments contain the greatest concentration of low performers?
- Does training influence performance?
- How closely is job satisfaction linked to employee performance?
- Does attrition differ between high and low performers?

Together, these questions reveal whether poor performance reflects individual ability or organizational investment.

Part II — The Burnout Deep Dive

The second section investigates whether the organization is unintentionally creating future low performers.

It explores:

- Workload versus compensation
- Overtime patterns
- Promotion frequency
- Salary equity
- Demographic differences
- Burnout indicators

The analysis shifts the conversation from *performance management* to *people management*, highlighting how organizational practices shape long-term employee outcomes.

## 📊 Key Insights

Several important findings emerged from the analysis.

📌 Training—not talent—distinguishes top performers.

Departments with lower performance scores consistently recorded lower training investment, suggesting that performance differences may reflect unequal access to development opportunities rather than differences in employee capability.

📌 Attrition alone does not explain performance.

Low performers were not resigning at substantially higher rates than other employees. Instead, turnover occurred across performance groups, indicating that employee retention cannot be understood through performance metrics alone.

📌 Burnout creates tomorrow's low performers.

Employees who resigned from Finance and Sales were among the organization's hardest workers. They logged the highest overtime hours, received relatively few promotions, and reported some of the lowest satisfaction scores.

Rather than losing disengaged employees, the organization appeared to be losing highly committed employees whose effort was not adequately recognized.

📌 Recognition matters as much as workload.

Burnout was associated not only with long working hours but with limited advancement opportunities and declining job satisfaction. Employees are more likely to disengage when sustained effort is not matched by organizational recognition.

## 💼 Business Value

This dashboard provides practical decision support for HR professionals, department managers, and organizational leaders.

It enables decision-makers to:

Identify departments requiring additional training investment
Detect early indicators of employee burnout
Prioritize retention efforts before turnover increases
Evaluate promotion and compensation equity
Improve workforce planning using evidence rather than assumptions

By shifting attention from reactive performance management to proactive workforce development, organizations can improve employee satisfaction while reducing the financial costs associated with turnover and disengagement.

## 📈 Methodology

The project followed a structured business analytics workflow.

1. Data Preparation

Employee records were cleaned and standardized in Microsoft Excel, including:

Missing value treatment
Data type corrections
Performance tier calculations
Feature preparation

2. Exploratory Analysis

Relationships between employee performance, training, satisfaction, compensation, overtime, promotions, and attrition were investigated.

3. Dashboard Development

Interactive Tableau dashboards were designed to answer business questions progressively rather than presenting isolated visualizations.

4. Business Interpretation

Analytical findings were translated into practical HR recommendations focused on retention, workforce development, and organizational performance.

## ⚠️ Limitations

This project is based on a publicly available employee performance dataset intended for analytical practice.

While the findings illustrate meaningful HR analytics techniques, they should not be interpreted as representing any specific organization's workforce.

The insights are designed to demonstrate analytical methodology, dashboard design, and evidence-based decision-making rather than provide operational recommendations for a real employer.
